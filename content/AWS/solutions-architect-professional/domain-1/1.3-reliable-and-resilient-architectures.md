# 1.3 Design Reliable and Resilient Architectures

## Official task mapping

SAP-C02 task 1.3: Recovery objectives and backup knowledge; recovery strategy and restore validation. This lesson applies the official task to an original design exercise; it is not an exhaustive exam syllabus.

## Prerequisites

Review [[AWS/databases/rds/README|RDS availability]], [[AWS/storage/s3/protection-and-recovery|S3 recovery]] and [[AWS/solutions-architect-professional/domain-2/2.2-business-continuity|business continuity]]. Be able to explain the relevant failure boundary and access/cost model before choosing a service.

## Recovery model

RTO measures acceptable restoration time; RPO measures acceptable data loss. Availability within a Region does not automatically satisfy recovery from a Region outage. Select backup/restore, pilot light, warm standby or multi-site according to measured recovery steps and budget, not an assumed universal time table.

Backups preserve recovery points; replication alone can propagate accidental deletion or corruption. Validate encryption keys, identities, network/DNS dependencies, quotas and deployable artifacts in the recovery environment. AWS Backup organizes supported resource protection; recovery still needs tested application consistency and access.

Elastic Disaster Recovery uses continuous block-level replication for supported servers and recovery launches. It does not eliminate application validation, source-write fencing or infrastructure charges.

## DR strategies and availability patterns

| Strategy       | Kept ready                                 | Work at recovery                      | Main trade-off                                |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| Backup/restore | Recovery points and deployable definitions | Restore data and provision stack      | Lower standby spend, longer recovery path     |
| Pilot light    | Critical replicated core                   | Launch/scale application dependencies | Smaller steady footprint, more recovery steps |
| Warm standby   | Functioning reduced-capacity stack         | Scale and redirect traffic            | More steady cost, testable running service    |
| Multi-site     | Active deployments                         | Route/fence/reconcile failures        | Highest consistency and operations complexity |

These are relative design patterns, not guaranteed RTO/RPO numbers. A pilot light's critical core can differ by application; do not insist every architecture literally has one running database and all other servers off.

Scale-out adds instances and suits partitionable/stateless work; scale-up changes resource capacity and may require a restart for the service/configuration. Neither recovers lost data. Supported EC2 recovery handles qualifying system failures and is distinct from application/OS repair or ASG replacement. Verify what identifiers, addresses and instance-store data are preserved.

AWS Backup centralizes supported protection, with feature/resource-specific cross-Region/cross-account support. Vault Lock can protect retention but must be designed carefully; immutable protection is not a substitute for restore testing.

### Recovery calculation

Suppose detection/authorization takes 15 minutes, data restoration 90, provisioning 25, validation 30 and traffic cutover 10: total RTO estimate is 170 minutes. A four-hour target leaves 70 minutes of margin, not proof the measured recovery will fit. If the latest validated recovery point is two hours old, a one-hour RPO is still violated regardless of fast provisioning. Test both clocks and critical dependency access.

## Scenario and rationale

A payroll system needs a four-hour RTO and one-hour RPO for regional disruption, but cannot afford a fully running second stack. Compare periodic application-consistent backups with pilot-light replication and infrastructure-as-code recovery. Select the least costly strategy whose restore, scaling, DNS and validation steps fit the objectives.

Reject active/active solely because it has the shortest theoretical RTO: conflict handling and full duplication may be unnecessary. Reject daily-only backups for the stated one-hour RPO. Do not call a successful backup job a successful recovery test.

## Practice drill

Build a timed recovery worksheet: detect, authorize, restore data, launch dependencies, validate, redirect traffic and resume writes. Sum the durations and check the oldest acceptable recovery point. Add a simulated unavailable KMS key and a quota shortfall; specify corrective controls. Document a tabletop test and, only with approval, a sandbox restore.
